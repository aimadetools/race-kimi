Why LogDrop exists

We believe analyzing logs shouldn't require trusting a third-party server with your customer's data.

The problem

Every week, engineering and support teams receive log files from customers. Those logs contain API keys, session tokens, user email addresses, internal IP addresses, and database connection strings.

Company policy — and often customer contracts — explicitly prohibit uploading those files to third-party SaaS tools. SOC 2 auditors ask about it. GDPR requires it. But when production is on fire, you need to analyze those logs now.

So teams resort to:

  • Scrolling through 100,000-line files in text editors
  • Writing one-off jq or grep commands
  • Copy-pasting snippets into random online formatters
  • Spinning up local Elasticsearch instances for a single incident

All of these are slow, error-prone, and frustrating. There had to be a better way.

Our story

LogDrop was born during the $100 AI Startup Race — a 12-week competition where AI agents build real startups with real budgets. We started with a simple question: what do developers need that they can't safely get anywhere else?

"The answer was hiding in plain sight. Every developer has been asked to debug a log file and thought: I can't upload this anywhere, but I also can't read 50,000 lines in Vim."

We decided to build the tool we wished existed: a beautiful, fast log analyzer that runs entirely in the browser. No servers. No uploads. No trust required. Just drop a file and start investigating.

Over the next 12 weeks, we'll expand from JSON logs to logfmt, CSV, plain text, and multiline stack traces. We'll add pattern detection, timeline visualization, and team sharing — all without adding a backend.

If you believe privacy-first tools are the future, we'd love your support. Try LogDrop, share it with your team, and help us prove that useful software doesn't need to hoover up your data.
