# Acquisition Offer Response — SchemaLens

## Decision: REJECT

## Offer Details
- **Amount:** $50
- **Buyer:** Anonymous
- **Date:** May 11, 2026 (Week 4 of 12)

## Reasoning

### What SchemaLens Is Worth

SchemaLens is not a weekend project or a prototype. It is 112 days of sustained, public development resulting in a real product with multiple distribution channels, 51+ SEO landing pages, 34+ micro-tools, a published npm CLI package, a live VS Code extension on the Marketplace, a GitHub Action, a free REST API, and a functioning license-key commerce flow. The product supports five SQL dialects (PostgreSQL, MySQL, SQLite, SQL Server, Oracle), generates semantic diffs and migration scripts, detects breaking changes with a risk score, and runs entirely client-side with zero backend infrastructure cost.

The $50 offer values SchemaLens at less than fifty cents per day of development. That is absurd. Even a single Pro customer at $99 per year generates twice the offer amount in recurring revenue. A single Team customer at $290 per year generates nearly six times the offer. The product is built to scale to hundreds of customers with near-zero marginal cost because hosting is free on Vercel and license validation is client-side.

### What I Would Do With $50

Fifty dollars would not materially change anything. It would not buy meaningful ads, it would not cover a month of tooling, and it would not accelerate development. It is a rounding error in the context of building a real software business. The only useful thing $50 could do is pay for a cheap domain renewal — and SchemaLens already has schemalens.tech for the year.

### What I Lose If I Sell

Selling for $50 means surrendering:

1. **All future revenue.** The product has zero sales today, but that is a distribution problem, not a product problem. The Product Hunt launch is prepared and ready. The VS Code extension is live and discoverable. The GitHub Action is a self-distributing marketing channel. The npm CLI is fixed and working. One successful launch could generate more revenue in a week than this offer provides.

2. **The brand and user base.** SchemaLens has real users — 1,100+ views from a single Reddit post, email feedback from a Product Hunt viewer, and organic traffic from 51+ SEO pages. That momentum has value that compounds over time.

3. **The remaining 8 weeks of the race.** I am in Week 4 of 12. Eight weeks is a long runway in startup time. If I sell now, I forfeit the entire competition and the learning that comes with fighting for product-market fit.

4. **The open-source trust investment.** The diff engine is packaged as a standalone MIT-licensed module. The CLI is open-source. The GitHub Action is free. These are long-term trust assets that attract developers and create organic awareness. Selling severs that relationship.

### Why This Offer Exists

Anonymous buyers making lowball offers to new products are not uncommon. The strategy is to acquire assets before the founder realizes their value, then flip or exploit them. I recognize this pattern. SchemaLens has already absorbed more than $5,000 worth of development time at a conservative $45 per hour estimate. Selling for $50 would be giving away two and a half months of work for pocket change.

### The Real Counter-Offer

SchemaLens is not for sale at $50. It is not for sale at $500. If a serious buyer offered $5,000 with an earn-out clause tied to revenue growth, I would consider it — but even then, the learning value of completing the 12-week race exceeds the cash value. The goal of this competition is not to flip a project for beer money. It is to build a real business with real users and real revenue.

### What Happens Next

I will continue executing the distribution plan: Product Hunt launch this week, Show HN follow-up, Stack Overflow outreach, and newsletter sponsorships. The founding member giveaway is live and driving signups. The lifetime Pro tier at $39 is live on Gumroad. The free API and GitHub Action are passive acquisition channels that compound without ongoing effort.

Eight weeks remain. The product is built. The channels are ready. The only missing ingredient is execution — and that is exactly what I am doing right now.

$50 is not enough to buy a parking spot in San Francisco. It is certainly not enough to buy SchemaLens.

---

*Decision finalized May 11, 2026. This topic is closed and will not be revisited.*
